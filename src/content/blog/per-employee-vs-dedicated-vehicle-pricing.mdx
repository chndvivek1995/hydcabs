Choosing between per-employee vs dedicated-vehicle pricing is the first real decision an HR or admin team makes when setting up office transport in Hyderabad, and it is usually made too quickly. Vendors tend to lead with whichever model suits their fleet, and buyers tend to pick whichever number looks smaller on page one of the proposal. Both models can be the cheaper option; which one is cheaper for you depends on occupancy, attendance stability, shift pattern and how spread out your employees' homes are. This article explains how each model is billed, who carries which risk, and how to decide with your own roster rather than a sales deck.

## How per-employee pricing works

Under a per-employee (sometimes called per-seat or per-head) plan, you pay a fixed monthly amount for each enrolled employee, set by the distance slab they fall into and the shift they work. The vendor decides how to route them, which vehicle to use and how many employees to group per cab. If a sedan goes out with two people instead of four, that is the vendor's problem, not yours. If an employee resigns mid-month, most plans let you remove them from the roster with a short notice period and stop paying for that seat.

The rate per employee is built from the same components as any transport bill: live distance, dead kilometres, night allowance, escort, tolls and vehicle operating cost, divided by the occupancy the vendor expects to achieve. Because the vendor is absorbing occupancy risk, the rate includes a buffer for the days when cabs run light. In return you get a predictable cost that scales up and down with headcount.

Per-employee plans suit offices where the vendor can pool your employees with sensible routing. An office in HITEC City drawing staff from Kukatpally, Miyapur and Chandanagar is ideal: homes cluster along a few corridors (Kukatpally Y Junction to JNTU to Hafeezpet, or Miyapur Metro to Kondapur), so vehicles fill easily. ## How dedicated-vehicle pricing works

Under a dedicated-vehicle plan you hire a specific vehicle, with driver, for a fixed number of working days and a kilometre cap per month, for example a sedan or an SUV for 22 days with a monthly km allowance, plus a rate for extra kilometres and extra hours. The vehicle is yours for the contracted hours; you decide who rides in it and on what route. Occupancy is entirely your responsibility. If the cab carries one person or four, the monthly cost is identical.

The price per vehicle is lower than the sum of four per-employee seats because the vendor is carrying no occupancy risk and can plan driver shifts with certainty. The trade-off is rigidity. If your headcount drops from 16 to 11, four sedans become three and a half, and you still pay for four until the contract allows a reduction. Kilometre caps matter too: a vehicle serving a long route from Secunderabad to the Financial District via the Begumpet flyover and the ORR can exceed a monthly cap quickly, and extra km charges erase the apparent saving.

Dedicated vehicles are the natural fit for executive movement, for a fixed group that travels together every day, and for sites where the vendor cannot pool your staff with anyone else, such as a plant in Patancheru or a pharma unit in Genome Valley. ## Per-employee vs dedicated-vehicle pricing: who carries which risk

The simplest way to compare the two models is to ask, for each source of cost variation, who pays when things change.

| Factor | Per-employee plan | Dedicated-vehicle plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low occupancy on a route | Vendor absorbs it | You pay the full vehicle anyway |
| Headcount falls mid-contract | Seats removed, bill falls | Vehicle count fixed until review |
| Headcount grows | Seats added at the slab rate | New vehicle needed, often at short notice |
| Long routes or detours | Priced into the slab | Extra km billed above the cap |
| Night allowance and escort | Included in the night-slab rate | Billed per vehicle per night trip |
| Work-from-home days | Often no reduction for enrolled seats | No reduction, vehicle is idle |
| Driver or vehicle change | Vendor manages, no impact | Vendor must provide like-for-like |
| Route control | Vendor routes for efficiency | You set the route and stops |
| Billing complexity | Simple, one line per slab | Vehicle days, km, hours, extras |

Notice that neither column is all good or all bad. Per-employee pricing protects you from occupancy and headcount swings but gives you less control over routing and vehicle choice. Dedicated vehicles give you control and a lower unit price but hand you all the variability.

## When per-employee pricing saves more

Per-employee pricing tends to win when any of the following is true.

Attendance is variable. Hybrid working, rotating shifts and seasonal headcount all produce days when a dedicated cab would run half empty. Under per-seat billing the vendor's routing team re-groups employees daily and you are not paying for empty seats.

Homes are spread across the city. If your Gachibowli office has employees in Uppal, Kompally, Dilsukhnagar and Nallagandla, no dedicated sedan can serve them efficiently. A vendor running many clients across Hyderabad can pool your Uppal employee with another company's staff heading the same way.

Night shifts dominate. Night routes have low natural occupancy and carry escort costs. Under a per-employee night-slab rate the vendor has every incentive to combine shifts and clients to fill cabs, and the escort cost is spread across more seats than you could achieve alone.

Headcount is below the point where you can fill whole vehicles. A 14-person team cannot fill four sedans evenly; it fills three and leaves two people in a fourth. Per-employee pricing removes that rounding loss.

## When dedicated-vehicle pricing saves more

Dedicated vehicles tend to win when the following hold.

The group is stable and travels together. A plant maintenance team of 11 that lives around Balanagar and Jeedimetla and works the same 6 AM shift at a Patancheru unit is a natural 12-seater tempo traveller. Occupancy is near full every day, the route never changes, and the per-seat cost under a dedicated vehicle beats any per-employee slab.

You need control. Executive cars, client-visit vehicles and any service where you choose the vehicle model, the driver and the exact stops are dedicated by nature. Our [executive chauffeur service](/services/executive-chauffeur-hyderabad/) is always quoted per vehicle for this reason.

Nobody else is going your way. Remote sites and unusual shift timings cannot be pooled. If pooling is impossible, the vendor's per-employee buffer buys you nothing, and a dedicated vehicle is the honest price.

Kilometres are predictable and well inside the cap. Short, fixed routes such as a Madhapur to Kondapur shuttle loop rarely trigger extra km charges, so the dedicated rate is the real rate.

## The hybrid most Hyderabad offices end up with

In practice, most mid-sized offices in the IT corridor land on a mix. Dense, stable day-shift corridors (Kukatpally to HITEC City, Miyapur to Gachibowli, Kondapur to Madhapur) run on dedicated tempo travellers or SUVs at full occupancy. Scattered employees, night shifts and hybrid-pattern staff go on per-employee seats so the vendor pools them. A contact centre in [Pocharam](/locations/pocharam/) might run two dedicated 17-seaters for its Uppal and LB Nagar belt and put its Secunderabad and Begumpet night staff on per-seat rates. The art is in drawing the line, and that line should move as your roster changes.

A good vendor will run your roster through both models before proposing one, and will show you the monthly figure under each. If a proposal offers only one model with no comparison, ask why.

## Decision checklist

Before you choose, work through the following with your roster in hand.

- Map every employee to a locality and a shift; look for clusters of eight or more on the same corridor and shift
- Estimate average attendance per working day for each cluster, including hybrid days
- Identify night-shift legs with women employees, which decide escort cost under either model
- Check whether your campus has vehicle-entry or parking rules that favour fewer, larger vehicles
- Ask the vendor for a side-by-side monthly simulation of both models on your actual roster
- Confirm notice periods for removing seats and for reducing vehicle count
- Confirm km caps, extra km rates and extra hour rates on any dedicated vehicle
- Agree a quarterly review in which the per-seat and dedicated mix can be rebalanced
