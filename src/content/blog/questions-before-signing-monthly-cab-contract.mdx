A monthly cab contract looks simple on paper: a fixed set of vehicles or a fixed set of employees, a monthly fee, a start date. The trouble is that the paper describes a quiet month, and Hyderabad does not supply many of those. There are weeks when the ORR is jammed after rain, days when a team in Gachibowli is asked to stay for a 10 PM release call, months with three public holidays, and the occasional morning when a driver assigned to the Miyapur pickup simply does not turn up.

The questions below are the ones we wish every company asked before signing with any vendor, including us. They are grouped so you can work through them in one or two meetings. Treat the answers as contract material: anything the vendor says yes to should appear in the agreement or its annexure. For context on how a monthly plan is typically structured, see our [monthly cab for office](/services/monthly-cab-for-office-hyderabad/) page.

## Questions about scope and kilometres

**1. What exactly does the monthly fee include?**
Ask for a written list: vehicle, driver, fuel, maintenance, insurance, permits, tolls, parking, and the kilometre and hour allowance for the month. Then ask for the list of exclusions. If either list is short or vague, keep asking.

**2. How is the kilometre allowance calculated, and from where?**
A dedicated vehicle contract usually carries a monthly kilometre cap. Find out whether the count starts at the driver's home, the vendor's hub or your first pickup. Dead kilometres, the distance a cab runs empty to reach the first employee, can be a significant hidden component on long belts such as Kompally to the Financial District.

**3. What happens when we exceed the cap, and how is it tracked?**
Ask for the extra-kilometre basis and, more importantly, how both sides will verify the reading. GPS trip logs are far better than odometer photos.

**4. How many working days does the month assume?**
Monthly packages are typically built on a set number of working days. Confirm how extra holidays, a six-day week during a project push, or an office shutdown are treated.

**5. What is the waiting-time policy?**
Employees run late, security checks take time, a Kondapur apartment gate is slow to open at 6 AM. Agree how long a cab waits at a pickup, what counts as billable waiting, and who decides.

## Questions about shifts and changes

**6. By when must roster changes be shared, and what happens to late changes?**
If your shift leads finalise rosters at 8 PM for a 6 AM pickup, the vendor needs to be able to handle that. Ask for the cut-off, the channel and the cost of a change after cut-off.

**7. How are no-shows defined and charged?**
There are two kinds: an employee who does not board, and a cab that does not arrive. The contract should define both, with a time threshold, and state the consequence for each side.

**8. How do you handle a shift extension or an unscheduled late drop?**
A team in HITEC City asked to stay back for a 2 AM US-client call needs a cab that was not on the plan. Find out how such requests are raised, the notice needed and how they are billed. Our [night shift cab](/services/night-shift-cab-hyderabad/) page explains how we approach late drops.

**9. Can we scale vehicles up or down, and with how much notice?**
Headcount moves. Ask for the change process, the notice period and whether per-vehicle rates shift with volume.

**10. What is the backup arrangement if a vehicle or driver is unavailable?**
Breakdowns, leave and illness are certain. Ask how quickly a substitute is deployed, whether it meets the same vehicle standard, and whether you pay anything extra when it is used.

## Questions about safety and drivers

**11. Will the same drivers serve our account, and how are replacements vetted?**
Consistency improves safety and punctuality. Ask whether drivers are dedicated to your routes and what verification a replacement goes through before the first trip. Our [safety page](/safety/) lists the checks we consider standard.

**12. What is your protocol for women employees on early or late trips?**
Ask the vendor to describe the sequencing rule, escort availability and drop confirmation without prompting. A vague answer here should be a deal-breaker.

**13. How do you monitor driving behaviour?**
Speeding on the PVNR Expressway at 3 AM is a real risk. Ask what the tracking system flags, who reviews it and what action follows.

**14. What is the incident and grievance process, with names and numbers?**
Get the escalation matrix in writing, including out-of-hours contacts.

## Questions about billing and reporting

**15. What does the monthly invoice look like, and what comes with it?**
Ask for a sample invoice and the annexure format. Trip-level data with dates, times and kilometres should be standard; our [technology page](/technology/) shows what a complete monthly pack contains.

**16. How are disputes on the invoice resolved, and within what time?**
Agree a review window and a process so that a disagreement on one trip does not hold up an entire month's payment.

**17. How and when can the rate be revised?**
Fuel-linked or annual revisions are common; the question is the formula, the trigger and the notice period. A contract that allows revision at any time with no notice is a problem.

## Questions about the agreement itself

**18. What are the lock-in and exit terms on both sides?**
Match any lock-in with a performance-based exit. Ask what happens if the agreed service levels are missed for a sustained period.

**19. What service levels are you willing to commit to, and what is the remedy?**
On-time percentage, escalation response times, document compliance. A vendor who commits to nothing is asking you to carry all the risk.

**20. What does the first month look like?**
Ask for the mobilisation plan: driver allocation by pickup belt, route trial, employee communication and the go-live sequence for each shift. The companion post in this cluster on switching vendors covers this in detail.

## A quick checklist before you sign

- Inclusions and exclusions are written as lists with worked examples.
- Kilometre basis, cap and verification method are defined.
- Working-day assumptions and holiday treatment are explicit.
- Roster cut-off, no-show definitions and late-change costs are stated.
- Women's night-shift protocol and driver verification are described in an annexure.
- Invoice format, dispute window and revision formula are agreed.
- Lock-in is matched by a performance-based exit.
- A named escalation matrix with out-of-hours numbers is attached.

If all eight are true, you are in reasonable shape. If more than two are missing, pause the signature and go back to the vendor.
