Switching transport vendors is one of the few admin projects where a mistake is visible to the whole office by 7 AM. The night shift that nobody picked up in Uppal, the US-shift team waiting outside a Gachibowli campus at 2:30 AM because the new driver had the wrong gate, the team lead in Madhapur fielding complaints before her first meeting. None of these are inevitable, but they all follow from treating a vendor change as a contract event rather than an operations project.

This post lays out a week-by-week transition plan built from what actually goes wrong during a changeover in Hyderabad. It assumes you have already selected the new vendor; if you are still at that stage, the vendor selection checklist and the RFP template in this cluster come first. Our [employee transportation](/services/employee-transportation-hyderabad/) page describes what a full-service vendor should bring to the mobilisation.

## The principles behind a clean switch

Before the checklist, four rules that shape everything else:

1. **Move by shift, not by date.** A single cut-over day concentrates every risk into one morning. Staggering shifts spreads it out and lets you learn from the first move before the harder ones.
2. **Secure your data before you give notice.** Roster, pickup points, route plans and trip history are yours. Get them out of the incumbent's systems while the relationship is still cordial.
3. **Run in parallel on the hardest routes.** The far ends of your pickup belts and the night routes are where a new vendor's gaps show. Shadow runs and overlapping coverage there are worth the cost.
4. **Over-communicate with employees.** Most disruption during a switch is confusion, not failure. People who know what to expect forgive a late cab; people who were not told do not.

## Week-by-week transition checklist

### Week 1: Decision, data and notice

- Confirm the new agreement is signed, with the mobilisation plan and go-live sequence written into it.
- Export from the incumbent, or from your own records: the full roster with pickup localities, shift timings and gender split per shift; current route plans and cab allocations; three months of trip-level history; the escalation log.
- Check the incumbent contract for notice period, handover obligations and any exit charges, and brief finance.
- Serve notice to the incumbent in writing, with a courteous covering note and a request for continued service through the notice period.
- Appoint a transition owner on your side with authority over shift leads and admin, and get the named counterpart from the new vendor.

### Week 2: Route study and driver mapping

- The new vendor runs a route study on your actual roster: cab count per shift, pickup clusters, estimated first-pickup times, dead-kilometre hotspots.
- Walk through the results together. Challenge anything that looks optimistic, especially the time allowed from the first pickup in Miyapur or Kompally to the office in the Financial District during the 8 AM peak.
- Agree the driver mapping by cluster and confirm the vendor's recruitment status for each. A cluster with no confirmed drivers yet is a risk flag.
- Fix the go-live order of shifts. A typical order: general day shift first, then the evening shift, then night and US shifts last.
- Share the site specifics: gate numbers, security process, parking, waiting bays, and the shift leads' contact numbers.

### Week 3: Vehicles, documents and systems

- Receive the vehicle and driver master for your account and audit it against the standards in your contract: permits, fitness, insurance, driver verification. Our [safety page](/safety/) lists what a complete file includes. Reject anything incomplete now, not after go-live.
- Set up tracking access for your transport SPOC and shift leads. If the vendor provides an employee app or SMS alerts, configure it for the first shift's riders. Our [technology page](/technology/) explains what to expect.
- Agree the roster-sharing channel and cut-off times with the new vendor, and test a dummy roster change end to end.
- Brief your security team on the new vehicles and drivers, and arrange ID or sticker recognition at the gate.
- Confirm the escalation matrix with names and out-of-hours numbers from the new vendor, and publish an internal version for shift leads.

### Week 4: Shadow runs and employee communication

- The new vendor runs shadow trips on the hardest routes without passengers: the earliest pickups, the longest belts, and the night-drop sequences. Drivers learn gates, apartment entrances and the realities of Kukatpally Y Junction at 7 AM.
- Review GPS traces from the shadow runs with the vendor and correct route plans.
- Send the first employee communication for the shift that moves first: dates, how to identify the vehicle, how pickups are confirmed, the escalation number. Repeat it through shift leads on the floor.
- Hold a joint session between your shift leads and the vendor's coordinators so faces and names are known before the first live morning.

### Week 5: First shift goes live, incumbent continues on the rest

- Move the general day shift to the new vendor. Keep the incumbent on every other shift.
- Have the vendor's coordinator and your transition owner physically present at the office for the first pickups and drops. Watch the far ends of the belts, not the easy pickups in Kondapur.
- Hold a short daily review for the first three days: on-time pickups, any no-shows, escalations raised and closed, employee feedback through shift leads.
- Fix route plans and pickup points on the same day issues appear. Small corrections in week 5 prevent larger ones in week 7.
- Keep a parallel backup arrangement with the incumbent for this shift for the first few days if the contract allows, or hold a couple of standby vehicles from the new vendor.

### Week 6: Evening shift moves

- Repeat the week 5 pattern for the evening shift. Evening drops run into dinner-hour traffic on the ORR and through Hitec City toward Kukatpally, so confirm drop windows have been adjusted from the day-shift plan.
- Begin shadow runs for the night and US-shift drop sequences with the women's safety protocol applied: who is picked first, who is dropped last, escort assignment, drop confirmation calls. Our [night shift cab](/services/night-shift-cab-hyderabad/) page sets out how we sequence these.
- Send the night-shift employee communication, with extra emphasis on how drops are confirmed and whom to call.

### Week 7: Night and US shifts move

- Move the night and US shifts last, once the day and evening shifts have run cleanly. Keep the incumbent available on the first one or two nights if possible.
- Have the vendor's night supervisor on site for the first 2 AM and 4 AM drop waves. Review every drop confirmation the next morning.
- Hold the daily review for the full week on these shifts, since they carry the highest safety stakes and the least traffic forgiveness if a route is wrong.

### Week 8: Close-out and first review

- Confirm the incumbent's final trips, reconcile their last invoice against trip data, and formally close the contract.
- Recover any access, badges, parking passes or stickers issued to the outgoing vendor.
- Hold the first full monthly review with the new vendor against the SLA metrics in your contract. The companion post on SLA metrics in this cluster lists the ones worth tracking.
- Collect employee feedback shift by shift and feed route corrections back into the plan.
- Document what went wrong and what you would do differently, because you will do this again in a few years.

## Common failure points, and how the plan prevents them

- **Drivers do not know the gates.** Shadow runs in week 4 and security briefings in week 3.
- **A pickup belt has too few drivers.** Cluster-level recruitment status in week 2, with a hard check before the shift moves.
- **Night sequencing goes wrong.** Night shifts move last, after shadow runs with the protocol applied and a supervisor on site.
- **Employees do not recognise the new vehicle.** Communication in week 4, repeated by shift leads, with vehicle identification details.
- **The incumbent slips during notice.** Data secured in week 1, invoices paid on time, new vendor ready to absorb a shift early.
- **Billing confusion in the overlap month.** Trip-level data from both vendors reconciled in week 8 before either final invoice is cleared.
