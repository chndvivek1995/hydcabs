Picking a corporate cab vendor in Hyderabad is one of those decisions that looks like procurement but behaves like operations. The contract is signed once; the pickups happen every day, at 6:30 AM in Kukatpally and at 2 AM outside a Gachibowli campus, in monsoon rain on the ORR and on long weekends when half the drivers want leave. Most of what goes wrong later was visible during selection, if someone knew what to look for.

This checklist is written for HR, admin and facilities managers who have been handed the job of choosing or replacing an employee transport vendor. It covers fifteen checks across compliance, safety, operations, technology and commercials. Use it as a scoring sheet during vendor meetings, or paste it straight into your evaluation template. If you want to see how a vendor should answer these questions, our [corporate cab vendor service page](/services/corporate-cab-vendor-hyderabad/) walks through our own operating model.

## Why Hyderabad makes vendor selection harder than it looks

The city's office geography is lopsided. Most corporate demand sits along the western IT corridor, from Madhapur and HITEC City through Gachibowli to the Financial District and Kokapet, while employees live everywhere from Miyapur and Chandanagar to LB Nagar, Uppal and Kompally. A single morning shift can mean cabs starting from Dilsukhnagar, crossing the city on the PVNR Expressway or the ORR, and converging on Mindspace within the same half-hour window.

Add rotating shifts for BPO and IT support teams, US-shift drops that finish between 2 AM and 4 AM, pharma units in Genome Valley and Pocharam with fixed plant timings, and the result is a vendor job that is more about scheduling discipline than about owning cars. The checks below are designed to test that discipline before you commit.

## The 15-point corporate cab vendor checklist

### Compliance and paperwork

1. **Business registration and GST invoicing.** Ask for the registration documents and a sample tax invoice. You want a vendor who issues proper monthly invoices with trip annexures, not a collection of handwritten bills from individual car owners. Confirm whose name will appear on the invoice when attached vehicles are used.

2. **Vehicle permits, fitness and insurance for every cab on your account.** Request a vehicle master sheet listing permit type, fitness validity, insurance validity and pollution certificate dates. A professional vendor keeps this current and can share it on demand. Ask how expiring documents are tracked and what happens to a vehicle whose cover lapses mid-month.

3. **Driver verification files.** Each driver should have a verified identity record, address proof, licence check and a background verification record on file, with a process for refreshing it. Ask to see three random files, not a curated sample. Our [safety page](/safety/) describes what a complete driver file looks like.

4. **Written incident and grievance process.** Who do you call at 3 AM when a cab has not reached a pickup in Nallagandla? Ask for the escalation matrix with named people and phone numbers, and the documented steps for an accident, a harassment complaint or a breakdown on the ORR.

### Safety practices

5. **Night-shift protocol for women employees.** The vendor should describe, without prompting, how routing changes after dark: women not picked up first or dropped last when travelling alone, escort availability, and confirmation calls on drop. Push for specifics, such as how a 2 AM drop to Miyapur is sequenced. Our [night shift cab service](/services/night-shift-cab-hyderabad/) page sets out the practices we follow.

6. **GPS tracking and trip visibility.** Every vehicle should be trackable in real time by the vendor's control desk, and ideally by your transport SPOC as well. Ask to see the live view during the meeting. If the answer is a WhatsApp group of driver locations, treat that as a gap.

7. **SOS and panic handling.** Find out what an employee does in an emergency, who receives the alert, and what the response time commitment is. Test it in the trial week.

8. **Vehicle hygiene and condition standards.** Ask for the inspection checklist drivers follow and how often supervisors physically audit vehicles. Hyderabad summers and monsoons are hard on cars; AC performance and wiper condition are not trivial comfort items.

### Operations and coverage

9. **Proven coverage of your pickup belts.** A vendor strong in Kukatpally and Miyapur may be thin in Uppal or Kompally. Share your roster postcodes and ask which belts they already run daily, and where they would need to recruit drivers. Location depth matters more than the city-wide claim; see our [HITEC City](/locations/hitec-city/) and [Gachibowli](/locations/gachibowli/) pages for the kind of route detail you should expect.

10. **Shift and roster handling.** How does the vendor receive roster changes, by when, and what happens to a change made at 9 PM for a 6 AM pickup? Ask how late-notice additions and cancellations are charged and how no-shows are recorded.

11. **Driver pool depth and backup vehicles.** Ask how many drivers are mapped to your account versus how many trips you run daily, and what the backup ratio is. A vendor with no slack will fail on the first festival weekend or during a monsoon flooding evening at Biodiversity Junction.

12. **Named on-ground supervision.** You want a transport coordinator who knows your building, your security team and your shift leads. Ask who that person will be and how many sites they already handle.

### Technology, billing and commercials

13. **Reporting and MIS.** Monthly invoices should come with trip-level data: date, shift, employee ID, pickup and drop time, kilometres. Ask for a sample MIS pack and confirm what dashboards your admin team can access. Our [technology page](/technology/) shows the kind of data a well-run operation produces.

14. **Transparent pricing structure.** Whichever model you choose, per-employee, per-trip or dedicated vehicle, the quote should spell out what is included and what is extra: dead kilometres, night allowance, escort, toll, parking, waiting time and km caps. Ask the vendor to model your actual roster so you compare total monthly cost, not headline rates. Our [pricing page](/pricing/) explains what drives cost.

15. **Contract terms that protect both sides.** Look at lock-in period, exit notice, SLA penalties and the mechanism for fuel-linked revisions. A vendor who refuses any service-level commitment is telling you something.

## How to use the checklist in practice

Run the fifteen points as a scored sheet, not a tick-box. Give each item a score from one to five and weight the sections according to your risk: a 24x7 BPO in Madhapur should weight safety and operations heavily, while a day-shift design studio in Jubilee Hills might weight commercials and vehicle quality more.

Do the document checks first, because they eliminate vendors quickly. Then hold a route-study meeting where the vendor presents how they would actually serve your roster: how many vehicles, which pickup clusters, estimated cab-fill, where the dead kilometres sit. A vendor who cannot do this without weeks of delay will struggle to adapt once you are live.

Finish with a paid trial on one shift for one or two weeks. Watch pickup punctuality at the far ends of your belts, say Kompally or Shamshabad, not just the easy ones in Kondapur. Track every escalation and how fast it closed.

## Red flags worth walking away from

- Quotes that are far below the rest of the field with no explanation of how the margin works.
- Refusal to share vehicle and driver documents before signing.
- No control desk you can call directly; everything goes through a single sales contact.
- Vague answers on women's night-shift sequencing.
- No written SLA, or penalties that are impossible to trigger.
- Invoices without trip-level annexures.
