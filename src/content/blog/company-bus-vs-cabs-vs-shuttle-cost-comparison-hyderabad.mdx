Every few months an admin head in Hyderabad does the same back-of-envelope sum: a bus carries 40 people, a cab carries four, so surely the bus is ten times cheaper per seat. Then the bus arrives, picks up 19 people, takes 90 minutes to wind through Kukatpally, and the sum falls apart. A company bus vs cabs vs shuttle cost comparison only works when you count total cost, which means occupancy, dead kilometres, employee ride time, flexibility and the overhead of running the thing, not just the rate per vehicle. This guide lays out all three models, the cost components that separate them, and the Hyderabad situations where each one genuinely wins.

## The three models, defined

### Point-to-point cabs

Sedans, SUVs and MUVs that pick each employee up at or near home and drop them at the office, usually grouping three to six people who live along the same corridor. Routes are rebuilt each day from the roster. This is the model behind most [office pickup and drop services](/services/office-pickup-and-drop-hyderabad/) in the IT corridor. It is the most flexible and the most door-to-door, and it is also the most sensitive to occupancy.

### Fixed-route shuttle

A tempo traveller (12 or 17 seats) or a mini bus (around 26 seats) running a published route with fixed stops and a timetable, for example Miyapur Metro to Kondapur to Gachibowli to the Financial District, several times across a shift window. Employees walk or ride to the nearest stop. The vehicle does not go to anyone's door. Our [employee shuttle service](/services/employee-shuttle-bus-hyderabad/) is built on this model.

### Company bus

A full-size bus (around 40 seats) on one or two long routes, typically once per shift start and once per shift end. It is the lowest cost per seat when it is full, and the highest cost per seat when it is not. It also demands the most of employees in terms of walking distance to stops and rigid timings.

## Why rate per vehicle is the wrong number

Three vehicles, three rates per kilometre; the bus is the most expensive per km and the sedan the cheapest. Divide by seats and the order flips. But neither per-km nor per-seat tells you the monthly cost of moving your actual employees. For that you need every component below.

Occupancy. A 40-seat bus running at 20 people costs the same as one running at 40, so its real per-seat cost doubles. Cabs are the opposite: a vendor re-groups them daily, so a sedan rarely goes out with one person. Shuttles sit in between, with occupancy that varies by run; the 8:30 AM run from Miyapur is full and the 10:15 AM run is not.

Dead kilometres. A bus parked at a depot in Jeedimetla serving an office in Kokapet covers a long empty leg before its first stop and after its last. Shuttles on a loop have very few dead km. Cabs dispatched from a hub near the pickup belt have a moderate amount, mostly on the first-pickup and last-drop legs.

Route length and time. A bus route that collects 40 people from Secunderabad, Begumpet, Ameerpet and Kukatpally before heading down to HITEC City is long, and the first person on board rides for a long time. That ride time has a cost in attrition and lateness that never appears on the transport invoice but does appear in HR's numbers. Cabs minimise ride time per person; shuttles are fast if stops are on a corridor and slow if they wander.

Flexibility. Cab rosters change daily at no extra cost. A shuttle timetable changes with a week or two of notice. A bus route change often means renegotiating the contract. Hybrid working and rotating shifts punish rigidity.

Night and escort rules. On night legs, women employees require first-pickup and last-drop handling and often an escort. One escort on a 17-seater shuttle is spread across many seats; one escort in a sedan is spread across three. Buses rarely run at night for most office profiles.

Overheads you carry. A company bus often comes with a transport coordinator managing stops, complaints and attendance, and with parking space at the office. Shuttles need stop discipline and a timetable someone owns. Cabs need the least internal effort if the vendor runs routing and tracking, which is where a [transport management solution](/services/transport-management-solution/) earns its place.

## Company bus vs cabs vs shuttle cost comparison at a glance

| Dimension | Point-to-point cabs | Fixed-route shuttle | Company bus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per seat when full | Highest | Middle | Lowest |
| Cost per seat at half occupancy | Rises a little (vendor re-groups) | Rises moderately | Doubles |
| Dead km exposure | Moderate | Low (loop) | High (depot legs) |
| Door-to-door | Yes | No, stop-based | No, few stops |
| Ride time per employee | Shortest | Short on corridors | Longest |
| Handles hybrid or variable attendance | Very well | Reasonably | Poorly |
| Night shift suitability | Good, with escort | Good on dense corridors | Rarely used |
| Change notice for routes | Daily | One to two weeks | Contract level |
| Internal admin effort | Low if vendor-managed | Medium | High |
| Typical headcount band on one corridor | Up to about 30 | 30 to 150 | 150 and above |

The headcount bands in the last row are a planning guide, not a rule; the right answer always comes from mapping where your people live.

## Where each model wins in Hyderabad

Cabs win for scattered teams and night shifts. A 120-person product company in Raidurg with staff across Manikonda, Kompally, Uppal and Nallagandla has no single corridor that fills a shuttle. Door-to-door cabs, pooled by the vendor with other clients, are the only model that serves everyone without 90-minute rides. The same applies to any US-shift team with 2 AM drops: cabs with escort rules are the standard. See how this plays out for a hub like [HITEC City](/locations/hitec-city/).

Shuttles win on dense corridors to a single office. A 300-seat office in the [Financial District](/locations/financial-district/) finds that a large share of its day-shift staff lives along the Miyapur to Kondapur to Gachibowli line, or along the Kukatpally to Hafeezpet to Gachibowli line. Two or three tempo travellers or a mini bus on each corridor, with stops at Miyapur Metro, Kondapur junction and the Gachibowli flyover, carry those employees at a lower per-seat cost than cabs and with ride times close to a cab. The remaining scattered staff stay on cabs.

Buses win for large, stable shifts at one site. A manufacturing or pharma unit in Patancheru, Genome Valley or Pocharam with several hundred employees on the same 6 AM or 2 PM shift, living in a few known belts such as [Kukatpally](/locations/kukatpally/), Balanagar and Jeedimetla, is the classic bus case. Attendance is high, timings are fixed and stops can be set at known junctions. Our [factory shift transport solution](/solutions/factory-shift-employee-transport-hyderabad/) describes how buses and tempo travellers are combined for plant rosters.

## The model most large offices end up with: trunk and feeder

Few offices above 200 seats run one model. The pattern that usually comes out cheapest is trunk and feeder. Trunk routes on high-density corridors run on shuttles or buses: Miyapur to Gachibowli, Kukatpally to HITEC City, LB Nagar to Pocharam via the Uppal Ring Road. Feeder cabs cover everyone the trunk cannot reach economically: scattered homes, night legs, late logins, and employees whose nearest trunk stop is too far to walk. Metro last-mile is a variant of the same idea: employees ride the Metro to Raidurg, Miyapur or LB Nagar and a shuttle or cab covers the final stretch.

The split should be revisited every quarter. A new residential belt filling up in Tellapur or Kollur can turn a feeder-cab cluster into a shuttle corridor within a year.

## Questions to settle before you choose

- Where do your employees live, by locality, and how many share each corridor and shift?
- What is your real average attendance per shift, including hybrid days?
- How far is acceptable for an employee to travel to a shuttle or bus stop, and will you support that with a stipend or a Metro pass?
- Do you have night legs with women employees, and how will escort rules apply under each model?
- Who will own stop discipline and complaints for a shuttle or bus, and what does that person cost you?
- Can the vendor show you occupancy and on-time data per route each month so you can rebalance?
- What is the notice period for adding or removing a shuttle run or bus route?
