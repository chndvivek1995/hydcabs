Working out the monthly cab cost for office commute in Hyderabad is harder than it looks, because no two offices buy the same thing. A 40-person startup near Raidurg Metro running a single 9 AM to 6 PM shift needs a very different plan from a 600-seat contact centre in Pocharam running US and UK shifts with 2 AM drops. Both ask the same question, "what will it cost per month?", and both deserve a straight answer. This guide explains every component that goes into that number, shows how the pieces combine, and tells you what to have ready so a vendor can give you a real quote instead of a guess.

We deliberately do not publish rupee rates in this article. Rates move with fuel prices, vehicle availability and the specific route, and any figure printed in a blog post would be wrong for your office within a quarter. What does not change is the structure of the bill. Once you understand the structure, you can compare any two quotes line by line. ## What goes into the monthly cab cost for office commute in Hyderabad

Every monthly transport invoice, whether it is quoted per employee or per vehicle, is built from the same eight inputs.

### 1. Distance slab

The biggest driver is how far each employee lives from the office. Most monthly plans group employees into distance bands, for example up to 10 km, 10 to 15 km, 15 to 20 km and beyond 20 km, measured as the road distance of the planned route rather than a straight line. In Hyderabad the slabs matter a great deal because the IT corridor pulls people from very different distances. An employee in Kondapur commuting to Mindspace in HITEC City sits in the shortest slab. A colleague coming from Kompally or LB Nagar to the same office is two or three slabs higher, and the ORR toll gets added on top.

### 2. Shift timing and night allowance

A general shift that starts around 9 AM and ends around 6 PM is the cheapest to serve. Once trips cross into late evening or early morning, a night allowance applies. The allowance covers the driver's higher wage for unsocial hours and the reduced number of trips a vehicle can complete overnight. For an office in Gachibowli running a US shift (roughly 6 PM to 3 AM), every drop leg falls into the night band. Rotational shifts that change weekly are priced on the mix across the month.

### 3. Escort

When women employees are picked up first or dropped last during night hours, a trained escort travels in the vehicle for those legs. The escort is a fixed cost per vehicle per trip, so it is spread across however many employees share that cab. On a well-filled route it is a small addition per seat; on a thinly loaded 1 AM route it can be a noticeable share of the trip cost. Our [women safety solution](/solutions/women-safety-employee-transport-hyderabad/) explains how routing is designed to keep escort legs to a minimum without compromising on the rule.

### 4. Dead kilometres

Dead kilometres are the distance a vehicle covers with no employee on board: from the vendor's hub to the first pickup, from the last drop back to the hub, and any repositioning between a morning and an evening run. They are invisible on the roster but very real on the bill. A cab based near Miyapur Metro that serves an office in the Financial District has a long empty return after the morning run unless it is given a second assignment. Dead km are usually bundled into the rate, but you should ask how much of the quoted km is live and how much is empty. We cover this in depth in our post on [cutting dead kilometres](/blog/dead-kilometres-employee-transport-bill/).

### 5. Tolls and parking

ORR tolls are the main item in Hyderabad. Routes that use the Outer Ring Road between, say, Nallagandla or Tellapur and Kokapet, or Shamshabad and the Financial District, will carry a toll on every trip. Parking charges arise mostly at campuses that levy a fee for vendor vehicles waiting during shift changes. Some vendors bundle tolls into the rate, others pass them through at actuals with receipts.

### 6. Vehicle type

A sedan, an SUV, an MUV and a 12-seater tempo traveller all have different operating costs per kilometre and different seat counts. The right choice depends on how many people share a route and how long it is. A tempo traveller serving 10 employees from Kukatpally to HITEC City via Hafeezpet costs more per vehicle than a sedan but far less per seat. See the [fleet page](/fleet/) for seat and luggage capacities by vehicle class.

### 7. Occupancy

Occupancy is the number of seats actually filled on each trip, and it is the single lever that most changes the per-employee cost. A four-seater sedan running with four people is twice as efficient as the same sedan running with two. Per-employee pricing bakes an assumed occupancy into the rate; dedicated-vehicle pricing leaves occupancy entirely in your hands. Attendance patterns, work-from-home days and shift rosters all affect it.

### 8. Contract length and working days

Longer commitments give the vendor confidence to dedicate vehicles and drivers, which usually earns a better rate. The number of working days the plan assumes also matters: a 22-day plan priced for a five-day office is different from a plan that must also run on Saturdays. Clarify how extra days, public holidays and planned shutdowns are billed.

## A worked illustration using variables

To show how the components combine, here is the arithmetic behind a single route, using labelled variables instead of real numbers. Replace each letter with the figure from your own quote.

| Component | Variable | How it is applied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live route distance (home to office) | D km | Measured on the planned route, one leg |
| Base rate for vehicle class | ₹A per km | Depends on sedan, SUV, MUV or tempo traveller |
| Dead km share | E km | Hub to first pickup and last drop back to hub |
| Night allowance | ₹N per trip | Applied only to legs in the night band |
| Escort | ₹S per trip | Applied only to qualifying night legs with women employees |
| Tolls and parking | ₹T per trip | ORR tolls, campus parking, at actuals or bundled |
| Working days | W days | Usually the office calendar, commonly 22 |
| Occupancy | P employees | Average seats filled on this route |

Per-trip vehicle cost is then (D + E) × A + N + S + T. Multiply by two legs (pickup and drop) and by W working days for the monthly vehicle cost. Divide by P to get the monthly cost per employee on that route. Two things jump out of this formula. First, E (dead km) is paid for by you even though nobody is in the cab. Second, P sits in the denominator, so every extra filled seat pulls the per-employee cost down more than any rate negotiation will.

## Three Hyderabad scenarios compared

The same formula produces very different outcomes across the city. Here are three patterns we see often, described qualitatively.

**Short-slab day shift, dense pickup belt.** An office near Cyber Towers drawing staff from Kondapur, Kothaguda and Madhapur. D is small, N and S are zero, tolls are nil because no ORR is involved, and occupancy is high because homes cluster along the Kondapur-Kothaguda road. This is the lowest per-employee cost profile in the city.

**Long-slab day shift via ORR.** A Kokapet office pulling employees from Uppal and LB Nagar. D is large, and T appears on every trip because the sensible route uses the ORR. Occupancy can still be good if the roster groups employees by locality. Cost per employee is noticeably higher, driven almost entirely by distance and tolls, not by the vendor's margin.

**Night shift, spread-out homes.** A Pocharam contact centre with a 1 AM drop for staff living across Secunderabad, Begumpet and Kukatpally. N applies, S applies on legs with women employees, occupancy is low because fewer people share any one direction after midnight, and E is high because vehicles return empty. This is the most expensive profile per seat, and also the one where good routing produces the biggest savings. ## What to send a vendor to get a real quote

A vendor who quotes without the following is guessing, and a guessed quote tends to drift upward after month one.

- Office address and the nearest landmark or junction, so the route study starts from the right point
- Employee home locations by locality or pin code (names are not needed at this stage)
- Shift timings for each shift, including login and logout windows
- Expected attendance pattern, including hybrid or work-from-home days
- Whether women employees will be on night legs, which decides escort requirements
- Preferred vehicle classes, if any, and any campus rules about vehicle entry or parking
- Contract length you are considering and the working-day calendar

With this information a route study can produce a per-employee or per-vehicle figure that holds through the contract, rather than a headline number that quietly grows. ## Common mistakes when comparing monthly quotes

Comparing the headline per-employee rate alone is the most common error. One vendor's rate may include ORR tolls, escort and dead km; another's may add them as separate lines. Always ask for the full monthly invoice simulation for your actual roster. Second, ignoring occupancy assumptions: a per-seat rate quoted at four per sedan will not hold if your attendance runs at two per sedan. Third, forgetting the working-day basis: a plan priced for 22 days looks cheaper than one priced for 26 days until you realise your Pocharam site runs six days a week.
