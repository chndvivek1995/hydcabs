The headline rate in a corporate cab proposal is rarely the number you end up paying. Hidden charges in corporate cab contracts are not usually buried in fine print; more often they are simply absent from the proposal and appear on the second or third invoice as extras that the vendor regards as obvious and the client regards as a surprise. For an admin or procurement team in Hyderabad, where ORR tolls, late-night shifts and campus parking rules create plenty of room for extras, knowing the full list before you sign is the difference between a contract that holds its price and one that drifts upward every quarter. This guide names the charges, explains why each exists, and gives you the clause to close it.

## Why extras appear at all

Most extras are not deliberate deception. They come from scope drift. The proposal was priced on a roster, a shift pattern and a set of routes; within two months the roster has changed, a new team has started a UK shift, and someone has asked for a Saturday run. The vendor incurs real costs for each change and bills for them. The problem is not that the costs exist; it is that the rate for each was never agreed, so the vendor sets it unilaterally after the fact. The fix is to agree every rate, including the ones you hope never to use, before signing. A vendor who is confident in its pricing will have no objection; see what we put in writing on our [corporate cab vendor](/services/corporate-cab-vendor-hyderabad/) page.

## The hidden charges in corporate cab contracts that surface after signing

### Extra kilometres beyond the cap

Dedicated-vehicle plans come with a monthly km allowance. Exceed it and every additional km is billed at a rate that is frequently higher than the implied base rate. A sedan serving a long route from LB Nagar to the Financial District via the ORR can exhaust a cap built for a Kondapur to Madhapur run within three weeks. Ask for the cap, the extra km rate and how km are measured (GPS log, odometer, or planned route).

### Extra hours and waiting time

If a vehicle is held beyond its contracted hours, for a late meeting or a delayed shift end, waiting time applies. Some contracts bill it per hour, others per half hour, and some have a free waiting allowance per trip. At shift changes in large campuses, where vehicles queue to enter, waiting time can accrue through no fault of yours.

### Night allowance

Trips in the night band carry a driver allowance. The band itself is the hidden part: one vendor's night starts at 10 PM, another's at 8 PM. A UK-shift drop at 10:30 PM may or may not attract the allowance depending on the definition. Get the hours in writing.

### Escort charges

Night legs with women employees on first pickup or last drop require an escort. The escort fee per trip is legitimate; the surprise is when it was never mentioned in the proposal because the proposal assumed a day shift. If any night work is possible during the contract, the escort rate belongs in the schedule. Our [safety page](/safety/) explains when escorts are deployed.

### Tolls and parking

ORR tolls are the big one in Hyderabad. A quote that says "excluding tolls" is honest; a quote that says nothing and then adds tolls at actuals plus a handling margin is not. Campus parking fees, where a tech park charges vendor vehicles for entry or waiting, behave the same way. Decide: bundled into the rate, or pass-through at actuals with receipts and no margin.

### Fuel price escalation

Many contracts include a clause that revises the rate when fuel prices move beyond a band. The clause is reasonable; the hidden part is a band so narrow that it triggers every month, or a formula that passes through more than the actual fuel share of operating cost. Ask for the base fuel price, the trigger band and the exact revision formula.

### Sunday, holiday and sixth-day runs

A plan priced on 22 working days does not cover a Saturday deployment or a public-holiday shift. Those days are billed as extras, often at a premium. If your office works six days, or if any team may be asked to work a holiday, the per-day rate for extra days must be in the contract.

### Cancellation and no-show fees

A trip cancelled after the vehicle has been dispatched costs the vendor the dead kilometres and the driver's time. A fee is fair. But the cut-off time (two hours before pickup, or at dispatch) and the amount need to be agreed, and the roster-update process needs to be good enough that cancellations are the exception.

### Vehicle upgrade or replacement charges

When the contracted sedan is unavailable and an SUV is sent, some vendors bill the SUV rate. When a vehicle breaks down and a replacement arrives late, some bill the replacement trip in full. The contract should state that a like-for-like replacement is at the contracted rate, and that an upgrade forced by the vendor's availability is not chargeable.

### Driver change and onboarding fees

A few contracts carry a fee each time a driver is replaced, framed as verification or onboarding cost. Driver verification is the vendor's responsibility and should be inside the rate.

### Technology and reporting fees

Live tracking, an employee app, monthly MIS reports and SOS features are sometimes quoted as a separate monthly platform fee per employee or per vehicle. There is nothing wrong with the fee if it is visible in the proposal. Check whether it is, and whether it rises with headcount.

### Airport and outstation movements

An ad-hoc guest pickup from Shamshabad RGIA or a day trip to a plant in Patancheru outside the monthly plan will be billed separately, usually under a different rate card with its own tolls, parking and driver allowance. Ask for that rate card at the time of the main contract, not when the first guest lands. The [airport transfer service](/services/airport-transfers-hyderabad/) and [Shamshabad](/locations/shamshabad/) pages describe what those movements involve.

### Minimum billing and lock-in exits

Some contracts bill a minimum number of seats or vehicles regardless of use, or impose an exit fee for terminating before the lock-in ends. Both can be acceptable in exchange for a lower rate, but they must be visible, and the minimum should fall if your headcount falls for reasons outside your control.

## Checklist: clauses to pin down before you sign

Use this as the schedule you attach to the contract. If a line is blank, the vendor sets the price later.

- Base rate per seat by distance slab and shift, or base rate per vehicle with working days and km cap
- Extra km rate and the measurement method
- Extra hour and waiting time rate, with any free allowance
- Night band start and end times, and the allowance per trip
- Escort rate per trip and the rule that triggers it
- Toll and parking treatment: bundled, or pass-through at actuals with receipts and no margin
- Fuel escalation base price, trigger band and formula
- Rate for Sundays, public holidays and sixth-day runs
- Cancellation cut-off time and fee; no-show fee
- Like-for-like replacement at the contracted rate; no charge for vendor-forced upgrades
- Driver verification and replacement included in the rate
- Technology and reporting fee, if any, and how it scales
- Airport and outstation rate card attached
- Minimum billing terms and exit conditions, with headcount-fall relief
- GST treatment of each line, including pass-through items

## The simplest test: a simulated invoice

Before you sign, give the vendor your real roster for one month, including the awkward bits: a UK-shift drop at 10:30 PM, two Saturday runs, one ORR route, a guest airport pickup and three late cancellations. Ask for the invoice that month would produce under the proposed contract, line by line. A vendor whose pricing is complete will produce it in a day. A vendor who says the extras will be handled as they come has told you exactly how the next twelve invoices will go. ## Hyderabad specifics worth naming

Three local features generate most of the extras we see in contracts across the city. ORR tolls, for any route touching Kokapet, Narsingi, Tellapur, Kollur, Shamshabad or the Nanakramguda exit, must be bundled or explicitly pass-through. Night belts for US-shift contact centres in Pocharam, Uppal and the Madhapur corridor make night-band definitions and escort rates material rather than theoretical. And campus entry rules at the larger tech parks in HITEC City, Gachibowli and the Financial District create waiting time at shift changes that neither party controls, so the waiting allowance needs to be realistic.
