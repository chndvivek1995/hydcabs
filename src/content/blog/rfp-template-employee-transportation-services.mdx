An RFP for employee transportation services does two jobs. It tells vendors exactly what you need so that every quote is built on the same roster, the same shifts and the same rules. And it forces your own team to decide things that otherwise surface as disputes six months later: who pays for a cab that waits twenty minutes in Kondapur, what counts as a no-show, how a 2 AM drop to Uppal is sequenced when one woman employee is on board.

This template is written for HR, admin and procurement teams at Hyderabad companies. It is structured so you can copy the outline into your own document, fill in the scope sections, and send it out. Where a section benefits from a local example, we have added one. If you want to see how a vendor should respond, our [employee transportation service page](/services/employee-transportation-hyderabad/) describes the operating model a complete bid should cover.

## RFP structure at a glance

| Section | What to include | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Introduction and objectives | Who you are, sites covered, what success looks like | Sets the tone and the evaluation lens |
| 2. Scope of services | Shifts, headcount, pickup belts, vehicle types, special needs | The core of every quote; vagueness here makes bids incomparable |
| 3. Service levels | On-time %, no-show handling, response times, reporting | Converts expectations into measurable terms |
| 4. Safety and compliance | Driver verification, vehicle documents, night-shift protocol, SOS | Non-negotiables that eliminate weak vendors early |
| 5. Technology and reporting | Tracking, employee app or alerts, MIS format, data access | Determines how much visibility your team gets |
| 6. Commercial model | Pricing basis, inclusions, exclusions, revision mechanism | Makes total cost comparable across bidders |
| 7. Transition and trial | Mobilisation plan, trial week, parallel run with incumbent | Protects shifts during the switch |
| 8. Contract terms | Tenure, exit, penalties, insurance, liability | Legal and finance review material |
| 9. Vendor profile and references | Experience in Hyderabad, fleet mix, team, references | Evidence behind the promises |
| 10. Submission and evaluation | Timeline, format, scoring weights, Q and A window | Keeps the process fair and on schedule |

The rest of this post walks through each section with notes on what to write.

## Section 1: Introduction and objectives

State who is issuing the RFP, the office locations involved and the broad goal. Be specific about outcomes, not adjectives. "Reliable transport" means nothing to a bidder; "pickups within the committed window for every shift, with full trip data for payroll and compliance" does.

If you have more than one site, say so here and flag the differences. An office in the Financial District and a plant in Pocharam have very different shift patterns and road access, and a vendor should know early that both are in scope.

## Section 2: Scope of services

This is the section that decides whether the quotes you receive are usable. Include:

- **Shifts.** List every shift with start and end times, including weekend and holiday patterns. A typical IT support roster might run a general shift from 9 AM to 6 PM, an evening shift ending at 11 PM and a US shift ending between 2 AM and 4 AM. Rotational patterns should be described, including how much notice employees get.
- **Headcount per shift**, with the split between men and women, because night-shift sequencing and escort needs depend on it.
- **Pickup belts.** Share localities or pin codes grouped by cluster: for example, a north-west cluster (Kukatpally, Miyapur, Chandanagar, Nizampet), a south cluster (LB Nagar, Dilsukhnagar, Vanasthalipuram), an east cluster (Uppal, Habsiguda, Tarnaka) and a central cluster (Ameerpet, Begumpet, Secunderabad). Vendors will use this to estimate route lengths and dead kilometres.
- **Office locations** with drop points, parking rules and security processes.
- **Vehicle expectations.** Sedans, SUVs, tempo travellers or buses, AC requirement, maximum age of vehicles, any EV preference. If you expect a mix, say what drives it. For fixed-route high-volume shifts, you may want to invite a shuttle option; our [employee shuttle page](/services/employee-shuttle-bus-hyderabad/) explains when that model fits.
- **Special requirements.** Accessibility needs, VIP or visitor movements, airport runs to Shamshabad, offsite events.

## Section 3: Service levels

Write the service levels you will actually measure. Common ones:

- On-time pickup and on-time drop, with the window defined (for instance, within a stated number of minutes of the scheduled time).
- Vehicle no-show or late-substitution rate.
- Escalation response and closure times, by severity.
- Report delivery timelines, such as trip annexures within a fixed number of working days after month-end.

Ask vendors to confirm each one and to propose the penalty or credit mechanism. If you want a deeper treatment of which metrics are worth tracking, see our note on SLA metrics in the same cluster.

## Section 4: Safety and compliance

List the documents you expect for every vehicle and driver, and how often they are refreshed. Ask for the written night-shift protocol for women employees, the SOS process, the incident response flow and the vehicle inspection checklist. Request that vendors describe how they handle a breakdown on the ORR at night, or a driver who fails a random check.

Make it clear these are pass-or-fail items. Our [safety page](/safety/) lists the practices a credible bidder should be able to evidence.

## Section 5: Technology and reporting

Specify what your team needs to see and when. At a minimum: live tracking for the control desk, trip-level data for billing, and a monthly MIS pack. If you want employee-facing alerts, OTP-based boarding or a dashboard for your admin team, say so, and ask vendors to demonstrate rather than describe. Our [transport management solution](/services/transport-management-solution/) page outlines the components you can ask for.

Also cover data handling: who owns the trip data, how long it is retained and how employee personal data is protected.

## Section 6: Commercial model

Pick a primary pricing basis and require every bidder to quote on it. Then list inclusions and exclusions you want addressed explicitly: fuel, driver wages, tolls, parking, dead kilometres, night allowances, escort charges, waiting time, extra kilometres beyond any cap, and festival or holiday operations. Ask how fuel-price changes are handled and what the notice period for any revision is.

Request the quote as a modelled monthly figure for your actual roster, with the assumptions shown. Our [pricing page](/pricing/) explains the factors that typically move that figure.

## Section 7: Transition and trial

Ask for a mobilisation plan with dates: driver recruitment per cluster, vehicle onboarding, route finalisation, employee communication and the go-live sequence by shift. Define the trial: which shift, how many days, which metrics you will track, and how the outcome affects the award.

## Section 8: Contract terms

Cover tenure, exit notice on both sides, service-credit mechanics, insurance expectations and liability. Flag any clauses your legal team requires. Keep this section short in the RFP and point to a draft agreement if you have one.

## Section 9: Vendor profile and references

Ask for Hyderabad-specific experience: which pickup belts they already serve daily, which shift patterns they run, the size and mix of the fleet available to your account, the names and roles of the operations team who would handle you, and two or three references you may call.

## Section 10: Submission and evaluation

Publish the timeline, the format you want responses in, and the scoring weights by section. Open a question window and circulate all answers to every bidder. A typical weighting puts operations and safety ahead of price, because the cheapest bid that fails on a monsoon evening at Biodiversity Junction costs far more than it saves.
